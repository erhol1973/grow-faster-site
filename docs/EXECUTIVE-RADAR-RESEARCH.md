# Executive Radar — research and editorial challenge, 13 November 2026

## Decision and scope

Prepare one short, dated decision agenda for CEOs of Belgian mid-sized businesses: where to investigate AI process value, when to wait on capacity spending, and which critical supplier dependency to test. These are editorial choices, not a measured ranking of Belgian CEO preferences. A source-checked current pilot is feasible; a claim of proven CEO utility is not.

Research window: **14 October–13 November 2026**. The three selected primary releases fall inside that window. Their publication dates are current; their underlying observations are older and explicitly identified. No real-time feed or scheduled updating is implied. As-of date: 13 November; proposed next review: 20 November. Human editorial ownership and sponsor acceptance remain open.

## Selection criteria, fixed before drafting

Five equal editorial criteria (1–5): audience relevance, Belgian meaning, concrete decision, evidential quality, brevity. Proposed admission threshold is 4 in each, with at most three signals and no filler. Scores are editorial estimates, not research findings, CEO ratings, predicted ROI or guarantees. A missing source, false freshness claim or unsupported causal claim blocks selection regardless of total score.

## What the audience evidence establishes

The two audience studies support *hypotheses* that execution/growth, capabilities and resilience deserve editorial attention. They do not establish how all Belgian CEOs prefer to read a radar, how frequently they want it, or whether this product changes their decisions. The target mid-sized segment is not adequately isolated. No precise general-population preference ranking is claimed.

Small, medium and large variants in the dataset are editorial scenarios. They are not sourced survey subgroups, interviews, simulated statements of named CEOs, or validation by Telenet, Proximus, KBC, Argenta, Acerta, TVH or GSK. Industry and regulation can matter more than size; a sponsor should not accept a size label as a substitute for that context.
