Decision rights for every agent

Autonomy only works when everyone knows who decides. Settle these five questions before an agent goes live.

QuestionOwnerRule
Who approves deployment?Business ownerNo agent goes live without a named business owner, after review by the agent owner and risk.
Who monitors its behavior?Agent ownerPerformance, drift and incidents are tracked continuously against agreed thresholds.
Who can intervene or stop it?Process ownerEvery agent has a human override and a stop switch that works in minutes, not weeks.
Who owns the handoff between teams?End-to-end process ownerAccountability gets lost in the transitions. One person owns the whole flow.
Who is accountable for the outcome?Business unit leaderOwning the model is not the same as owning the decision it influences.

Final accountability stays singular and human.

Through the coherence lens

Agents expose unclear roles at machine speed. If people do not know today who decides, an agent will not solve that. It will escalate into a void.

The goal is not maximum autonomy. The goal is the right autonomy for the right risk, without governance becoming the brake itself.

  • ProportionalLight controls for low-risk agents, deep controls for critical decisions.
  • Paved roadsApproved, reusable patterns replace case-by-case approval.
  • PrunedA control that no longer reduces risk is removed, not kept by habit.

European rules shape agent autonomy

  • EU AI ActHigh-risk AI systems need effective human oversight. Organizations that deploy them must assign competent people to monitor them and to intervene.
  • GDPR, Article 22People have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that significantly affects them. Build a meaningful human review into those decisions.
  • Belgium: CBA No. 39When new technology has significant collective consequences for jobs, work organization or working conditions, employee representatives must be informed and consulted in advance.
  • Agent-specific risksPrompt injection, overly broad tool permissions and errors that compound across steps. Give every agent least-privilege access, full logging and testing before go-live.

A summary for executives, not legal advice.

Through the coherence lens

Examine which controls complement one another and which repeat the same check. Assess their contribution to risk management alongside their effect on the work.
